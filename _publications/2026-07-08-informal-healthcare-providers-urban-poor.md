Informal healthcare providers — unregistered and informally trained — play a significant role in rapidly urbanizing settings. Drawing on experience from Bangladesh, Ghana, Nepal and Nigeria, this paper shows that they bridge care gaps for disadvantaged urban populations, often serving as the first point of contact and delivering services ranging from preventive to curative care. At the same time, they operate without standardized protocols, formal credentials or professional accreditation. The paper argues that initiatives integrating informal and formal sectors show promise for improving the availability and quality of healthcare for the urban poor.
